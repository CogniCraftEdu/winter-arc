6.4  Motion in a Plane

When a body is projected into the air at some angle to the horizontal, it follows a curved path called a trajectory. The only force acting on it, if air resistance is neglected, is its weight, which acts vertically downward with acceleration g.

The motion is therefore resolved into two components: a uniform motion along the horizontal and a uniformly accelerated motion along the vertical. The initial velocity u is resolved as

ux = u cos θ,   uy = u sin θ

At any instant t the displacement components x and y are found by applying the equations of kinematics independently to each direction. The horizontal acceleration ax = 0 whereas ay = −g.

x = u cos θ · t
y = u sin θ · t − ½ g t²

Eliminating t between the two equations we obtain the equation of the path, y = x tan θ − g x² / (2 u² cos² θ), which is of the form y = ax + bx² and represents a parabola.

Solved Example 6.7. A ball is thrown at 20 m s⁻¹ at an angle of 50° above the horizontal. Find the time of flight, the maximum height reached and the horizontal range.

Time of flight  T = 2 u sin θ / g

H = u² sin² θ / 2g
R = u² sin 2θ / g

The maximum horizontal range is obtained when θ = 45°. For complementary angles the ranges are equal. At the highest point the vertical component of velocity is zero, while the horizontal component remains u cos θ throughout the flight.

It is important to note that the equation of motion in each direction is independent of the other, a result known as the principle of independence of motion; see Section 3.9 for the corresponding treatment of relative velocity.
